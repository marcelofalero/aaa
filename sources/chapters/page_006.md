# CHAPTER 1: FAST-PLAY RULES

## What is Roleplaying?

If you're familiar with other roleplaying games (like the AD&D® game), you might want to skip to the next section: "Roleplaying Alternate Tomorrows," because we're about to provide a quick overview of what a roleplaying game is. If you're new to the hobby, get ready to enter an exciting new world.

Remember when you used to play "Make Believe," when you pretended to be "Cops and Robbers™" or "Cowboys and Indians"? For a few hours on a summer afternoon, you played the role of a police officer or a cowboy or some other hero from comic books or television. You created props and settings from your imagination, and you and your friends made up stories that everyone contributed to. It was lots of fun, but it was also totally free-form—there were no rules to speak of, and sometimes disagreements cropped up that ended a game before a full story could be told.

Make Believe has come a long way. It has matured and developed into modern roleplaying games such as this one. In a roleplaying game, every player takes on the role of an imaginary character. These player characters are the heroes of the story the group is collectively going to tell.

One member of the group, however, takes on a special role. That person is the Gamemaster (or GM). The Gamemaster uses the rules with his judgment to decide by applying the results of or by gauging the circumstances to specific situations. The Gamemaster sets all players of each story the background, describes the characters who make up the supporting cast and villains, planning encounters that get the action moving. From that point on, the GM (much as the director or the narrator in a movie) guides the heroes through the world and describing the action as it unfolds. The Gamemaster is in charge of the game and keeps it going. A simple game of Make Believe might halt, it's usually because the players couldn't agree on what happened or what would happen next. This discussion between the players, much like a movie, the players are the ones who do. The players take an active role.
