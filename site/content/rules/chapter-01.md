# Fast-Play Rules

This chapter provides the basics of the **Alternity®** game system. It gives you enough material to get a feel for the game mechanics, so you can participate in an adventure and learn the bare bones of the system.

## Key Concepts

As a player, your primary job is to portray one of the **characters** in the story. The characters are the stars of the unfolding adventures that make up a Gamemaster’s ongoing campaign.

### Important Terms
- **Gamemaster (GM):** The participant who acts as the moderator, creating adventures and representing all "other characters," including friends and enemies.
- **Setting:** The environment for the adventure (modern technothriller, near-future mystery, or far-future space opera).
- **Adventure:** A scenario where characters interact with the supporting cast to solve a problem.
- **Campaign:** A series of connected adventures where characters grow and change.

## Science Fiction Characters

In Alternity, characters are defined by their **Profession**, **Abilities**, and **Skills**.

### Professions
- **Combat Spec (Combat Specialist):** Warriors who rely on physical power, endurance, and battle tactics.
- **Diplomat:** Negotiators, managers, deal-makers, or clergy who use interaction skills and resolve.
- **Free Agent:** Field operatives and troubleshooters who rely on agility, intuition, and independence.
- **Tech Op (Technical Operative):** Skilled in the use, creation, or maintenance of high-tech equipment.

### Abilities
Every character has six primary Abilities, with scores typically ranging from 4 to 14 for humans:
- **Strength (STR):** Physical power and prowess.
- **Dexterity (DEX):** Agility, coordination, and reflexes.
- **Constitution (CON):** Overall physical fitness and toughness.
- **Intelligence (INT):** Mental quickness and learning ability.
- **Will (WIL):** Mental fortitude and intuitive capacity.
- **Personality (PER):** Social abilities and charisma.

### Action Check
An Action Check determines how soon your character acts in a round. The result depends on Dexterity, Intelligence, and a profession bonus.
- **Degrees of Success:** Marginal, Ordinary, Good, and Amazing.

### Durability
Durability tracks damage sustained during an adventure:
- **Stun Points:** Bumps and bruises (equal to CON).
- **Wound Points:** Serious injuries (equal to CON).
- **Mortal Points:** Deadly trauma (equal to half CON, rounded up).

## Playing the Game

The core mechanic of Alternity is simple: Roll a number equal to or less than your character's score to succeed.

### The Most Important Rule
**Not every action requires a dice roll!** The GM only calls for rolls in dramatic situations where the outcome is in doubt.

### Rolling Dice
The Alternity game uses two kinds of dice:
1.  **Control Die:** Always a 20-sided die (**d20**).
2.  **Situation Die:** Varies from **d0** (none) to **d20** (d4, d6, d8, d12, or d20).

**Checking for Success:**
- Roll the control die and the situation die together.
- **Minus Die (-d4, -d6, etc.):** Subtracted from the control die (helpful).
- **Plus Die (+d4, +d6, etc.):** Added to the control die (penalty).
- If the total is **equal to or less than** your character's score, the action succeeds.

### Degrees of Success
The lower you roll, the better you succeed:
- **Critical Failure:** A roll of 20 on the control die (regardless of situation die).
- **Failure:** Total roll is greater than your score.
- **Ordinary Success:** Total roll is equal to or less than your full score.
- **Good Success:** Total roll is equal to or less than half your score.
- **Amazing Success:** Total roll is equal to or less than one-quarter your score.

### Situation Die Steps
The **Situation Die Steps Scale** determines which die to use. Each "step" moves the die size up or down the scale (d4, d6, d8, d12, d20).
